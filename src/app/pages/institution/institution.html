About Tarksanhita

Tarksanhita is an independent centre for structured debate and applied legal reasoning, founded on the conviction that sound law rests on sound logic.

The Centre convenes members, practitioners and scholars to test legal questions through disciplined argument, adjudicated debate and published research.

Our Mission

To convene arguments that are made with evidence, answered with reason and decided with integrity.

Our Vision

To become India's foremost forum where law, logic and public reasoning meet.

About Tarksanhita

Tarksanhita — the Compendium of Reasoning — is a centre for law, logic, debate and knowledge. The Centre convenes motions, hears the arguments made on them, and publishes the reasoning that follows.

Everything here is organised around the argument. Motions are published in advance, debated under recorded rules, decided by adjudicators who must give written reasons, and archived with the papers and commentary they provoke.

MotionsLive legal and policy controversies, published in advance.
MethodFormal and informal logic applied to legal reasoning.
ForumMoot courts, competitions, lectures and conferences.

Our Mission

To convene jurists, scholars and citizens who reason from evidence, argue in good faith and hold their own conclusions to the same scrutiny they apply to others'.

The Centre pursues this through four commitments: motions open to any member willing to prepare; research that is published, cited and contestable; debate conducted under published rules and neutral adjudication; and a reference library open to every member without gatekeeping.

RigourNo claim without a source; no source without scrutiny.
AccessConcessional membership and open digital resources.
IntegrityPublished conflict-of-interest and ethics norms.
} @case ('vision') {

Our Vision

To become the foremost Indian forum where law, logic and public reasoning meet — a place practitioners consult, scholars publish in, and arguments are settled on merit.

Over the coming decade the Centre intends to build a fully open case-law and bare-act repository, host an annual national conference on legal reasoning, and place a trained adjudicator in every affiliated debating society in the country.

} @case ('excellence') {

Standards of Debate

Motions are drawn from nine subject benches and argued in four settings: moot court, policy debate, public debate and the adjudicated competition round. Each has a published rulebook covering speaking times, burdens, evidence and points of order.

Adjudicators are drawn from the bar, the bench and the Centre's fellows. Every panel publishes written reasons, every decision may be appealed to a review panel, and the adjudication criteria are published before a motion opens.

Legal Research

Research is organised into standing centres for constitutional studies, criminal justice, technology & law, and human rights. Each runs a working-paper series, an annual call for papers and a public lecture series.

The Centre operates a double-blind peer review process for its two flagship journals and maintains an ethics committee that clears all empirical work involving human participants.

} @case ('debate') {

Debate & Logic

Debate is not a sideline here — it is the whole of the work. Members prepare argument construction, fallacy identification, burden allocation and rebuttal, then take them into adjudicated rounds decided against published criteria.

Motions are drawn from live legal and policy controversies. Adjudicators publish written reasons, and every decision may be appealed to a review panel — the same discipline the Centre expects of courts.
