Procedure

How a Motion Is Decided

The format is deliberately strict. It is what separates a debate from an argument.

  1. I

    Declare a position

    Read the motion and its definitional note, then declare for or against. The position is recorded before any argument is filed.

  2. II

    File the argument

    State the claim, give the reason it follows, then cite the evidence for the reason. Assertions offered without support are marked as such.

  3. III

    Answer the other side

    A rebuttal addresses a specific argument, not the member who made it, and is threaded beneath the point it answers.

  4. IV

    Adjudication

    Adjudicators publish written reasons against the criteria in the rulebook. Any decision may be appealed within fourteen days.

Have a motion in mind?

Write it as a proposition someone could reasonably oppose — that is what makes it debatable.

Rules of Engagement

The Forum Charter

Four rules, enforced by moderators and by the adjudication panel. Breaches are removed, not argued with.

Read the Rulebook
  1. Attack the argument Address what was said. A submission that characterises the member rather than the claim is withdrawn without notice.
  2. Cite what you claim Statute, judgment, data or source. A point with no support may still be filed, but it is weighed as an assertion.
  3. Read before you rebut Rebuttals are threaded beneath a specific argument. Restating your own case is not a reply to theirs.
  4. Concede when you are wrong A recorded concession costs nothing and is respected. Holding a defeated position costs the debate its point.
